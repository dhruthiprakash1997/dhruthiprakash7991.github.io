Nest: No Parent Should Do This Alone

An AI-guided platform that matches single parents into real-time peer support and shared parenting activities — built for the MEMPC Hackathon Finals 2026 and placed 2nd runner-up representing Duke against other MEMPC universities.

AI Product Design 2nd Runner-Up · MEMPC Finals 2026 Consumer Social / Wellbeing Go-to-Market Strategy
→ View the live prototype at nest-parent-support.base44.app
Exhibit E — Engagement Context

15.9 million single parents, zero co-pilot

Built for MEMPC Hackathon Finals 2026 (theme: Change the World × New Age PM), Nest starts from a simple insight: single parents handle every decision, hard night, and school emergency entirely alone, with no product designed for real-life support, companionship, or shared parenting. The research the team leaned on was blunt — the absence of peer support, not income or education, is the primary driver of poor mental health in single parents. One trusted peer in a similar situation changes everything.

Exhibit F — Product

Four moments, one AI-guided response

OVERWHELMED

A 5-minute AI-matched voice call with a parent in the exact same moment — no scheduling, no prep.

PLANNING

An AI day builder plus swap finder for pickups and weekly coordination with parents nearby.

COORDINATING

Need matching, a resource finder, and emergency circle activation for when it can't wait.

NOT ALONE

Activity matching, parallel play, and a weekend planner — because weekends are the hardest part.

How it works: Share a moment → AI matches → show up. No scheduling apps, no waiting — Nest finds the right match and the right moment so the hardest part is already done before the app even opens.

Exhibit C — Competitive Landscape

Every existing app solves a fragment

Frolo, Amicable, Care.com, Peanut, and Nextdoor each solve one slice — community, co-parenting logistics, paid childcare, mom social, or neighborhood trust — but none combine real-time help matching, an AI core, activity hosting, and a trust system built for US single parents specifically. Zero products stack all four. That's not a niche gap; the pitch argued it's the entire problem.

Exhibit S — Go-to-Market

Seed one city. Prove it. Scale the proof.

Phase 1 · Months 1–6

150 parents, one city

  • Distribution through school PTAs, WIC offices, and pediatric clinics
  • Target: 5+ peer connections built per parent
Phase 2 · Months 7–15

500 users via peer referral

  • First employer partnership activated
  • First documented proof that peer support reduces isolation
Phase 3 · Months 16–24

2,000+ users, 3–5 cities

  • First B2B employer contract signed
  • Public health grants plus measurable isolation reduction

The strategy is deliberately geographic-density-first: one parent using Nest alone has zero value, so the network effect is the product, not a side benefit.

Exhibit P — Business Model

Free to connect, Nest+ to go deeper — revenue follows proof

The free tier covers help requests, need matching, activity and group participation, and a basic profile with the community "Wall of Joy." Nest+ at $4.99/month adds priority AI matching, a personalized weekly AI digest, unlimited chat with a verified badge, and advanced hosting tools — but by design, Nest+ only launches once Month 3 retention exceeds 35%, so monetization follows validated engagement rather than the other way around.
