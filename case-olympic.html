Case 12 · Marketing Analysis

Olympic Rent-A-Car: Navigating Loyalty in a Competitive Market

A 5C analysis of Olympic's loyalty program economics — whether matching Enterprise's free-day rewards is worth the cost, and how to grow membership profitably.

Marketing Analysis Team Project 5C Framework Loyalty Program Economics
Exhibit 5C — Market Snapshot

Where Olympic stands today

COMPANY

Founded by an Olympic pole-vaulting champion. Positions itself as the value-conscious option for both business and leisure travelers, priced below competitors.

CUSTOMER

Frequent business travelers, airport shuttle renters, and downtown city renters, followed by leisure travelers.

COLLABORATORS

Major airports, downtown locations in major cities, and small regional franchise partners.

COMPETITION

Enterprise is the largest rival by market share and revenue, with Hertz and Avis close behind.

CONTEXT

A new competitor reward program threatens Olympic's revenue, and 3rd-party consolidators are shifting consumer focus from loyalty to price.

Exhibit C — Strategic Recommendations

A 4Ps plan to grow loyalty profitably

PRICE

Dollar-based rewards

Bonus points and holiday promotions for existing members, plus special offers for leisure travelers.

PROMOTION

Focus on high-value travelers

Direct marketing at business travelers and high spenders, and fix complaints about a confusing rewards experience.

PRODUCT

Simplify the program

A more mainstream, user-friendly system that's clearly differentiated from competitors' reward structures.

PLACE

Extend distribution

Partner with hotels and travel destinations, and integrate with 3rd-party consolidators to capture leisure travelers.

Final recommendation: roughly double loyalty-program investment — from $28M to ~$56M — projected to capture about $90M of the incremental profit opportunity.
