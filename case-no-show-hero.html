No Show Hero: Filling the Gaps in the Booking Calendar

Product strategy for a last-minute booking platform helping salons, spas, and other service providers turn canceled appointment slots into revenue.

Product Strategy Team Project Marketplace Consumer + SMB
Exhibit E — Engagement Context

Turning a cancellation into a booking

Salons, spas, and similar service businesses lose real revenue every time a client cancels a slot too late to refill it the normal way. No Show Hero connects those last-minute openings with nearby customers looking for a same-day or next-day appointment — a two-sided marketplace problem with a narrow, urgent window to work with.

Exhibit R — Scope of Work

Research grounded in both sides of the marketplace

The team ran 23 stakeholder interviews across 6 countries — 13 customers and 10 service-provider business owners, ages 21–65, through a mix of in-person and online sessions — then mapped both sides onto empathy maps and personas before touching a single feature idea. That grounding, paired with a competitive scan of the booking and marketplace landscape (Booksy, Fresha, StyleSeat, Mindbody, and others), shaped a set of product strategies built around how providers and customers actually behave under time pressure.

Exhibit F — Pain Points

Five jobs-to-be-done, from both sides of the marketplace

VISIBILITY

Providers need a way to promote and advertise their services across multiple platforms so they gain more reach.

ONE ROOF

Customers need to manage and research all their service needs in one place, without coordinating across separate platforms.

LAST-MINUTE ACCESS

Customers need to find last-minute openings and communicate with providers fast enough to book the same day.

CLUNKY UI/UX

Customers are re-entering the same personal details with every provider — friction that costs bookings and leads.

FRAGMENTED INBOX

Providers need every booking query, across every platform, in one place — or they miss appointments and leads.

Exhibit F — Areas of Focus

Five product bets that convert cancellations into revenue

01

All-in-one mobile application

A single service-management app for booking, managing, and tracking appointments — replacing the scatter of platforms customers were juggling.

02

CRM service desk + scheduling calendar

A lightweight CRM and calendar so providers handle cancellations, rebooking, and customer service from one screen instead of five inboxes.

03

Marketing automation tool

Lets providers push promotions across social, search, and booking apps from one place, closing the visibility gap the interviews surfaced.

04

Product-supplier partnerships

In-salon discount counters and sample products from partner brands — an added revenue and engagement layer beyond booking fees.

05

At-home services via gig workers

A fallback for last-minute cancellations that can't be filled in-salon, dispatching a gig-worker provider to the customer instead.

Exhibit P — Path Forward

Where the work goes from here

Success gets tracked across four lenses — engagement, efficiency, satisfaction, and revenue recovery for providers — alongside a risk-mitigation plan covering data security, provider vetting for the at-home channel, ad-policy compliance for the marketing tool, and clear vendor terms for partnership products. Porter's Six Forces analysis flagged low barriers to entry and intense rivalry from established players (Booksy, Fresha, StyleSeat, Mindbody) as the biggest threats — the mitigation is differentiation through the unified last-minute layer these competitors don't offer.
